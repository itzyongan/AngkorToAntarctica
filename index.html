From Angkor to Antarctica

The first Cambodian Gen Z South Pole expedition. Taking Cambodian youth to the bottom of the world.

Back the expeditionRead the mission

A mission, not a holiday

We are a generation of young Cambodians who believe our ambitions should have no geographical boundaries. We want to take Cambodia to one of the most extraordinary places on Earth, and return with a message about our shared responsibility for the future of our planet.

Youth achievement

Show that Cambodian young people can aspire to, plan and accomplish extraordinary international challenges.

National representation

Put Cambodia on the map in an environment where it is rarely represented.

Environmental awareness

Give the journey a climate-change and environmental education dimension.

Cultural exchange

Document the expedition and share it with Cambodian school and university students.

The honest cost of the Pole

Skiing the final 111 km to the geographic South Pole is far more demanding, and far more expensive, than flying there. Published prices for the “Last Degree” are in the range below, before international flights, personal kit, insurance and preparation.

111 kmThe last degree of latitude, on skis

Ninety days to the start line

The campaign runs as a public event, with a launch in Phnom Penh and an international push led by every participant as an ambassador.

  1. Days 1–20

    The announcement

    A short film introduces the team, the challenge and why Cambodia belongs at the South Pole.

  2. Days 21–40

    The public challenge

    A fundraising event in Phnom Penh for universities, businesses and media. The first corporate partners are announced.

  3. Days 41–65

    The international campaign

    The crowdfunding page opens worldwide, aimed at the diaspora, alumni and climate and youth organisations.

  4. Days 66–90

    The final push

    Progress reports, sponsor interviews, training footage, educational videos and a closing event.

What Cambodia gets back

Every supporter is investing in something that stays home. When the team returns, they will deliver:

  • Presentations at 20 Cambodian schools and universities
  • A short documentary about the expedition
  • A social media series on climate change and Antarctica
  • A youth leadership programme built on resilience, teamwork and international ambition
  • A photographic exhibition in Phnom Penh
